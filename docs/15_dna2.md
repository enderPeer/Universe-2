# DNA-2: three fixes to DNA-1, where they were made, how they were checked, and the second run (2026-10-10)

[docs/12](12_dna1_seeded_world.md) measured why DNA-1 cannot sustain a lineage: its only intact copier (7 codons)
stops copying when its head meets a nonzero cell in the *neighbourhood* of the child slot, which is there in 30 to
75 % of placements, and after one division its template pointer P points past the gene for good, so fecundity is
exactly 1 and every seeded population decays geometrically to extinction. DNA-2 is DNA-1 plus three changes, all
behind a `--dna2` flag so that every DNA-1 result stays reproducible bit for bit.

## 1. The three fixes

| # | change | why | exact rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | new operation **TERM** on the codons TTA and TTG (taken from Leu; COPY keeps CTT, CTC, CTA, CTG); under `--uniform-code` on codon value 21 (CCC), which was the first NOP | a copy loop needs an exit that depends on the copy's progress, not on the surroundings: the polymerase's terminator | skip the next codon **unless** the template has just passed a STOP codon: with o = P mod G, the test is true when o >= 3, o mod 3 = 0, o / 3 <= maxk + 1 and the codon formed by cells P-3, P-2, P-1 (read under the snapshot rule) is a STOP codon of the active table; if it is false, pc += 1 |
| 2 | **DIVIDE re-arms the template** | after DNA-1's first division P pointed past the gene, so the same loop could never copy again (fecundity 1) | a successful DIVIDE also sets P = start of the own slot |
| 3 | **COPY and COPYR do nothing while no child is claimed** | needed for fix 2: in DNA-1 they still advanced P and Q, so P drifted during the tick in which the next ALLOC waits for its grant and the second copy started misaligned | if child < 0: no write, P and Q unchanged |

Why "has just passed a STOP" rather than docs/12's literal "skip if the codon at P is STOP": that test fires when P
reaches the STOP, *before* it is copied. The child would then have no STOP, its gene would run on into whatever the
child slot held, its gene hash would differ from the parent's, and no copy could ever be faithful. Testing the three
cells behind P fires right after the STOP has been copied. The codon alignment (o mod 3 = 0, counted from P's slot
start) keeps three cells that happen to read T A A across a codon boundary from ending a copy early.

With these, the 5-codon gene `START ALLOC COPY TERM DIVIDE` (e.g. `ATG GCT CTT TTA GGT`, plus its STOP) is a
replicator: ALLOC claims a slot; COPY, TERM (skipping DIVIDE) loops one cell per pass; after the STOP is copied
TERM falls through to DIVIDE; P is re-armed and the next pass claims again. 18 cells at about 5 per tick, so a
child every 4 to 5 ticks for the whole 64-tick life.

## 3. Verification

- **DNA-1 unchanged.** Without `--dna2` the reference reproduces DNA-1's test hashes (t1 after 200 ticks:
  `a8dc45022d3a32d1`, as in docs/10), and the CUDA world engine matches the reference on DNA-1 t1 and t3 on every tick.
- **World engine against reference, DNA-2**, every tick hashed: blank and soup worlds, mu 0, 0.001, 0.005 and 0.01,
  uniform code, single strand with 64 damage hits per tick, 1,024-cell slots, an unseeded random world. **9 of 9
  configurations identical on every tick** (2,300 tick hashes). In the seeded ones the replicator spreads: 751 to 1,130
  births, 883 to 1,111 of them faithful, lineages 10 to 17 generations deep within 300 ticks.
- **Sweep engine against reference, DNA-2**: 200 random genes of 2 to 7 codons per set plus 75 designed genes (every
  order of ALLOC, COPY, TERM, DIVIDE in two spellings each, with 0 to 2 random codons inserted) or 72 uniform-code
  spellings: standard 64 ticks, standard 256 ticks, soup background, uniform code twice. **901 genes, 108,512 tick
  hashes, 0 mismatches**, and the record counters (claims, copied cells, births, faithful, deaths, live) agree.
- adler40's NVIDIA user-space driver was updated on 2026-10-10 without a reboot, so `nvidia-smi` fails there with a
  version mismatch; CUDA itself works (a DNA-1 world on the 4090 gave the reference's hash `1d4728689644696d`). GPU use
  on adler40 could therefore not be checked with nvidia-smi before the run; the process list showed no other GPU job.

### Phase 2: seven worlds of 99,999,744 cells, 50,000 ticks each (`results/dna2_pilots/`)

Seeded runs: 4,096 copies of `ATG GCT CTT TTA GGT` (`AAA AGA AAG CCC AGC` under the uniform code) in random soup,
inflow and damage at their defaults, double strand unless stated. One RTX 3060 each, 386 to 1,575 ticks per second.

| world | alive (second half, min to max) | births | faithful share | max lineage depth | distinct genes at the end |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,024-cell slots, mu 0.001 | 88,712 to 89,196 of 97,656 slots | 70,632,712 | 98.37 % | 1,542 | 1,185 |
| 256-cell slots, mu 0.001 | 355,305 to 356,220 of 390,624 | 282,504,257 | 98.37 % | 1,545 | 3,090 |
| 1,024-cell slots, mu 0.01 | 86,064 to 86,818 | 68,947,788 | 84.13 % | 1,542 | 8,036 |
| single strand, damage x 10 | 88,591 to 89,084 | 70,645,367 | 98.26 % | 1,538 | 1,518 |
| uniform code | 88,655 to 89,133 | 70,616,253 | 98.15 % | 1,536 | 113 |
| **unseeded random soup** | **7 to 90,793** | 8,690,847 | 62.17 % | 213 | 3,571 |

Every seeded population holds the world for all 50,000 ticks, about 1,540 generations (32 ticks per generation),
at 91 % of the slots. Under the standard code the most common genes at the end are all **synonymous spellings** of
the seeded five operations (`ATG GCT CTG TTA GGA`, `ATG GCC CTT TTA GGT`, ...): mutation explores the code's
redundancy freely and the operation sequence stays fixed. Under the uniform code there is no synonym to drift into,
so one spelling holds 95 % of the population and the variants are the seed with one extra codon appended (a mutated
STOP). The single strand with ten times the damage changes almost nothing here, because damage hits a cell once in a
million per tick while every organism is rewritten by copying every 32 ticks.

**The unseeded world produced a replicator by itself.** For 43,300 ticks it looked like DNA-1's random world: 7 to
51 organisms alive, every birth spontaneous, nothing faithful. Between tick 43,299 and 43,399 a gene that arrived
with the inflow copied itself faithfully: `ATG ATC TTA GGA AAG ATA AGG CTT AGC GCG GAT`, START SKNZ TERM DIVIDE R3
SKNZ L1 COPY R1 ALLOC L3, 11 codons, a different arrangement from the hand-written seed. Its descendants occupied
70,312 slots by tick 43,899 and 90,000 by tick 44,700, and held the world to the end (84,375 alive, 213
generations deep, 5,402,831 faithful births). The most common gene at tick 49,999 is a descendant with its own
operation sequence, START ALLOC TERM DIVIDE R3 COPY COPY COPY R1 SEEK L3. This is the first origin of a
self-sustaining replicator from random material in any of the four projects. One run, one seed: the waiting time
(43,350 ticks at 24 refilled slots per tick, about a million random genes) is a single sample.

The population holds 91 % of the slots from tick 200 to the end (2,666,893 to 2,670,391 alive in the second half),
98.34 % of 25.4 billion births are faithful, and the deepest lineage is 17,923 generations old. DNA-1's world of the
same size and length (docs/10): 3 faithful births, no lineage beyond depth 1.

Evolution in this world is neutral drift through the code's synonyms. In all 3,000 censuses the ten most common
genes have the seed's operation sequence START ALLOC COPY TERM DIVIDE; what changes is the spelling. At tick 199 the
seed spelling `ATG GCT CTT TTA GGT` holds 2,672,060 organisms; at the end the top ten spellings hold about 25,000
each (`ATG GCG CTG TTG GGG`, `ATG GCG CTG TTG GGC`, ...), and 6,798 distinct genes coexist. Nothing in this world
selects for anything but copying, and the five-codon seed is already the fastest possible copier, so no new
operation sequence can win. That is the expected result for a world without tasks, and the baseline for one with them.

## 7. Reading

1. **The two changes docs/12 derived from DNA-1's failure were the right ones.** A copy loop that ends on the
   template's STOP and a template pointer re-armed by DIVIDE turn DNA-1's one sterile 7-codon copier into a machine
   whose shortest replicator has 5 codons and where one 7-codon gene in 6,400 founds a lineage. Every replicator found
   has grandchildren; DNA-1's never did.
2. **Populations persist at every scale tested**: 50,000 ticks at 10^8 cells under six conditions, and 600,000 ticks
   at 3 x 10^9 cells with lineages 17,923 generations deep and 98.3 % faithful births.
3. **Replication arises without a seed.** The unseeded world produced a self-sustaining 11-codon replicator after
   about 43,350 ticks, roughly a million refilled slots, which matches the density this sweep measures for short
   genes. One run; the rate needs many seeds to pin down.
4. **The genetic code's degeneracy multiplies replicators by about 60 to 130**, at every length measured (5, 6 and 7
   codons). The standard code's synonyms give the copy machinery several spellings each, so random strings hit a
   working combination far more often.
5. **Without a task the world only drifts.** Copying is the only thing rewarded and the 5-codon seed is already the
   fastest copier, so evolution in the big world moved through synonyms and never changed the operation sequence.
   Selection for anything else needs the next layer: tasks and energy, docs/08 layer 4 and the protein layer of the
   function search.
